What this is

The gap between what students are taught about communications and what the profession looks like on the ground is wide, and it is getting wider.

Tech & Comms is The Comms Boardroom's attempt to close that gap early, while students still have time to act on it. It is not a lecture series. It is a set of straight conversations, led by working practitioners, built around three things every Mass Communication student should hear before they graduate.

Most curricula were designed for a version of the industry that no longer fully exists. Students leave fluent in theory but with little exposure to how communications works inside a real organisation, how much technology now shapes the work, or how they are expected to present themselves professionally. The information exists. It usually arrives after graduation. We would rather bring it while students are still curious and still have room to experiment.

Vision

Graduates who are ready for the profession as it is.

A generation of Nigerian communications graduates who walk into their first role already fluent in how PR, data and technology work together.

Goal

Close the gap before graduation, not after.

Take working practitioners into Mass Communication departments, one campus at a time, so students meet media intelligence, value measurement and personal branding while still in school.

Approach

Conversations, not lectures.

Practitioner-led sessions with a live Q&A, run with departments, alumni bodies and institutions that want students prepared for real practice.

Key topics · session running order

Three conversations every student deserves.

01

Communication: school vs reality

What brand and business communications really looks like in Nigeria, and where technology is changing it.

Textbooks describe communications as one fixed thing. It isn't. The work changes with the organisation, the industry and especially the country you practise in. This session gives an unfiltered picture of the job as it is done today.

02

Media monitoring & media intelligence

Monitoring, evaluation and social listening are already part of PR. Learn how practitioners prove their value.

Technology isn't a future trend in this industry; it is already here. Proving communications and PR value is one of the biggest conversations in the industry globally, across Africa and in Nigeria. Students who never meet media intelligence in school can't join that conversation, let alone help solve it.

03

Building your professional presence for attracting opportunities

Start telling the story of who you're becoming, and get your profiles ready for opportunities.

Personal branding isn't about having arrived. It is about documenting the process of getting there. The story worth telling isn't "I made it"; it is "here's who I'm becoming". The session covers that mindset and the practical basics of getting your profiles in order.
